## Data Vocabulary

### Barcode Count

The enhancer data uses two column names depending on source:

- `n_barcodes` in learn-ready tables.
- `number_of_barcodes` in raw fastqs1-5 Lib1 tables.

In these experiments, barcode count is used as the measurement-support / replicate proxy for each enhancer construct. Most splits treat higher-barcode rows as higher-confidence observations.

## Split Vocabulary

The split names are easy to blur together, so these are the working meanings:

- `hq_first`: validation/test are drawn from high-quality rows; training starts from leftover high-quality rows and only adds lower-quality rows after the HQ pool is exhausted. Good for optimistic high-quality performance, but train size and quality composition are confounded.
- `random`: train examples are sampled randomly from the eligible pool.
- `random_all_per_seed`: validation and test are random from the full data per seed, not restricted to HQ rows. This is a harder, more AL-realistic check.
- `random_hq_val_test_per_seed`: validation/test are drawn from high-quality rows per seed; training examples come from the remaining pool under the requested barcode threshold or bin.
- `random_mixed_train_disjoint_test_quality_per_seed`: training uses a capped mixed-quality pool; test is disjoint and balanced across explicit barcode-quality bins.

For `Lib1EnhancerDataModule` scratch runs, `test_min_barcodes` controls which rows are eligible for validation/test. Training eligibility is controlled by `train_min_barcodes`.

- `B2_with_RC` is a strong default and is often as good as weighted `B3`.

- Random-all split is a stricter AL-readiness test and produces lower R2, so do not compare it naively to HQ-first/HQ8 results.

Barcode interpretation:

- Barcode count matters, but "more barcodes is always better for training" is too simple.
- Exact 3-barcode examples were surprisingly useful in the exact-low-bin study.
- Exact 1- and 2-barcode examples are not worthless, but they are noisier and usually weaker than exact 3 or mid/high bins.
- HQ8 heldout splits produce stronger and cleaner metrics, which likely reflects a cleaner test target, not only a better model.
- When train bins are equalized by sample count, B2 with RC is a strong default; barcode-weighted B3 has small or inconsistent advantages.
- For team discussion, separate two questions:
  - what barcode count is acceptable for training data?
  - what barcode count is acceptable for validation/test or go/no-go claims?

- Use BODA2/Malinois transfer, not from-scratch enhancer training.
- Keep `B2_with_RC` as the default baseline.
- Include `B3_with_RC_weighted_bcap_10` or cap 8 as a secondary arm when barcode weighting is biologically important, but do not assume it will dominate.
- Prefer `full` and `conv3_plus` when heldout validation is clean enough; keep `branched_only` as a robust low-capacity comparator.
- Track rank metrics (`Spearman`, top-K enrichment, precision/recall on top observed sequences) alongside R2/COD, because AL use may care more about ranking than calibrated activity.

Recommended split defaults:

- For model-selection claims to the team: use a fixed or per-seed HQ8 validation/test split and state that it is a high-confidence target estimate.
- For AL deployment realism: use random-all or quality-resolved test splits and state that these are harder, mixed-quality performance estimates.
- For barcode policy: report exact low-bin and comparable-bin results separately from mixed-train quality-resolved test results.

Open questions to settle before the next team share:

- Which split is the canonical "go/no-go" split for the next AL cycle?
- Are 1- and 2-barcode examples allowed into training by default, or only after weighting / filtering?
- Should validation/test be HQ8-only for model selection, with quality-resolved test bins as a secondary diagnostic?
- Do we deploy a single best model or an ensemble/disagreement scorer across init heads and seeds?
- Which notebooks should be committed as canonical analysis surfaces versus kept as local exploratory scratchpads?
